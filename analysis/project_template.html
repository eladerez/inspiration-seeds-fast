Inspiration Seeds, Faster

How much faster can Inspiration Seeds run before it stops being creative? We ran eight configurations (the baseline and seven speedups) on one RTX PRO 6000, on the paper's full benchmark, and measured speed, how non-literal each blend is, and how different the four seeds of a pair stay.

The question

Speedups exist. What do they cost a tool built for exploration?

Inspiration Seeds (Goldberg, Richardson and Vinker) blends two images into a new object without a text prompt. It runs FLUX.1 Kontext [dev] with a LoRA, about 34 s per image on an L40S, and the paper names speed as a limitation. Quantization, step caching and few-step LoRAs all speed up FLUX, but they are usually judged on image quality. A tool for ideation also needs its blends to stay non-literal and its seeds to give different ideas, so that is what we measured.

Same benchmark as the paper

The public 99 pairs from 41 images, 4 seeds each, the paper's prompt, canvas and guidance. 396 images per configuration.

Creativity

The paper's metric: how many words Gemini needs to describe how to rebuild the output from its two inputs. Scored twice per configuration.

Diversity

Mean DreamSim distance between the 4 seeds of a pair. If a speedup makes seeds agree, you get fewer ideas per pair.

Paired statistics

Each configuration is compared with the baseline over the same 99 pairs, with 95% bootstrap intervals.

Takeaways

What to use

  • Same output, faster: 4-bit NVFP4 (2.4×). No loss in creativity or diversity (seed diversity even rose 3%); images stay close to the baseline's.
  • Much faster, nearly the same: NVFP4 + step caching (4.9×). −2.8% description length and −3% diversity, both real but small.
  • Fastest: NVFP4 + 8-step Turbo-Alpha LoRA (7.5×). Blends stay as non-literal, seed diversity drops 7%.

The paper's ~34 s per image was measured on an L40S, a different GPU: compare our speedups with our own baseline (20.91 s on the RTX PRO 6000), not with the paper's time. Limits: Gemini 3.8 Flash scored creativity because the paper's Gemini 2.5 Flash is closed to new users, so word counts compare with our own baseline (51.5) rather than the paper's 54.8.
