Inspiration Seeds, Faster

Inspiration Seeds (FLUX.1 Kontext + a LoRA) blends two images into a new, non-literal object. We made it faster seven ways and checked what each speedup did to the blends: how creative they are (Gemini's description length, the paper's metric) and how different the four seeds of a pair stay (DreamSim).

What to take away

Findings and limits

  • No speedup made the blends more literal. Description length moved by at most 1.4 words, and copy, insertion and split stayed rare.
  • Cheaper steps are free. FP8 and 4-bit NVFP4 cost neither creativity nor diversity (NVFP4's seed diversity even rose 3%).
  • Fewer steps cost diversity. The 8-step LoRAs keep each blend non-literal but make the seeds more alike (6–9% lower), and that cost is the same with or without 4-bit weights.
  • Recommended: config 4 (4.9×) keeps diversity almost intact; config 7 (7.5×) is the fastest if a 7% diversity loss is acceptable.
  • The paper's ~34 s per image was measured on an L40S, a different GPU: compare these speedups with our own baseline, not with the paper's time. Limits: the paper's scorer (Gemini 2.5 Flash) is closed to new users, so we used Gemini 3.8 Flash and compare to our own baseline. Copy/insertion/split use our own keyword rule.
